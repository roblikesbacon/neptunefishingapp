Enrich

Every catch arrives pre-loaded with its weather.

Nobody writes down the barometer reading every time they land a fish. You type in two things; the moment you save, Neptune looks up the conditions at that spot and time and stores another fourteen next to them.

  • Weather: pressure and its trend, air and water temperature, wind, cloud cover, UV, precipitation, moon phase and illumination.
  • Tide: stage, height and minutes to the next change, from real NOAA station data.
  • People: day of week, weekend and holiday flags, and a human-traffic index, because crowds move fish too.

Learn

It works out what actually produces your fish.

Most apps count the conditions you caught fish in, which mostly shows when you go fishing. Neptune measures fish per hour fished against your own average, so the hours you blanked count too.

  • Eight factors, each drawn as a curve across its whole range with the current reading marked.
  • It raises the bar for every pattern it checks, keeping the odds of even one false finding around 1 in 10. Three findings at most.
  • Most of the time it says nothing. Until a pattern is both real and worth acting on, the card says it hasn't found one yet.

The part no other logbook does

How old is that fish, and is it prime?

You caught a 19½-inch brown trout. Neptune works out that it hatched in 2020, that six is right in the prime years for a brown, and that the same batch was stocked into three other waters within driving distance.

  1. Length becomes age.

    Biologists publish growth curves. Neptune runs one backwards: give it a length, get an age.

  2. Age becomes a hatch year.

    The year you caught it, minus its age.

  3. Hatch year meets stocking records.

    Batches born within a year of your fish, searched across every water in the state.

  4. Prime, or not.

    39 species carry a curated trophy age window. Inside it, the fish is flagged PRIME.

Looked up, or left blank. Never invented.

Most age windows come straight from state and federal fisheries research: walleye 8-15 years, muskellunge 8-15, where the average one an angler catches is 11 years old and 45 inches.

Where no published trophy age exists, Neptune works the window out from the species' growth curve and tells you so. No window means no badge, never a guess. Bait species like Golden Shiner are left out on purpose.

Read a water's future, not just its past.

If a lake got a heavy 2020 stocking and that species peaks at ages 6-12, you know something concrete about the next few seasons there.

Find the same fish somewhere quieter.

The same batch of fish, the same age, often went into water nobody is crowding.

Bite Window counts the hours you caught nothing.

Other bite charts plot when you caught fish, which mostly shows when you go fishing. Bite Window plots fish per hour fished, so blank hours pull the line down like they should. Then it lets you read that rate four ways:

  • The sunHours from sunrise and sunset
  • The tideHours from high or low, near a real station

An engine that says “I don’t know”.

Neptune would rather tell you nothing than something that sounds right and isn't.

  • A pattern that could still be chance makes no claim at all, however many catches are behind it.
  • An unpublished stocking count stays blank. It's never counted as zero.
  • A stocking record without the fish's age is never matched to a year class.
  • An uncurated species gets no PRIME badge, not a guessed one.
  • A missing forecast value drops its factor from the score, silently.
