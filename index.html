The walkthrough

One trip, end to end.

Neptune's whole argument is that a logbook should get smarter the more you use it. Here's the actual path a single catch takes — from two taps on the riverbank to a statement about a fish's age you couldn't have made yourself.

01 Log

Two taps on the water, not twenty.

Start a live trip and Neptune runs a timer, holds your location, and keeps a running tally while you fish. Logging a fish is a species, a length, and you're back to casting. Breaks pause the clock. Everything writes to the device first, so a dead zone on the river never costs you an entry — it syncs when you come back into signal.

  • Live trip tracking with elapsed time, breaks, and a per-catch GPS pin.
  • Offline-first writes. Log with no bars; sync reconciles later.
  • Four ways in from the ⊕ button: log a catch, start a trip, log a past trip, or plan one.

02 Enrich

Every catch arrives pre-loaded with its weather.

This is the part nobody wants to do by hand, and it's the part that makes analysis possible later. The moment a catch lands, Neptune attaches the environmental snapshot around it — pulled from public weather, water, and tide services, not typed in by you.

You logged a species and a length. Neptune stored fourteen more dimensions alongside it.

  • Auto-captured: barometric pressure and its trend, air and water temperature, wind speed and direction, cloud cover, UV index, precipitation, moon phase and illumination.
  • Saltwater aware: tide stage, tide height, and minutes to the next tide change, from real NOAA station data.
  • Pressure you can't measure: day of week, weekend and holiday flags, and a human-traffic index — because crowds move fish too.

03 Learn

It works out what actually produces your fish.

Here's the turn — and the part most fishing apps get quietly wrong. Counting the conditions you caught fish in measures when you went fishing, not when fish were biting. Neptune divides by effort instead: every number is catches per hour fished, measured against your own season-adjusted baseline.

So a bin on the pressure curve doesn't say "you caught 38 fish here". It says your catch rate in that band beat your own average, by enough, with an interval that doesn't cross "no effect". Blank hours count against it. That's a much harder claim to make — and a much harder one to fool yourself with.

  • Eight factors: barometric pressure and its trend, water temp, air temp, wind speed, cloud cover, precipitation, and moon illumination — each drawn as a response curve across its full range, with the current reading marked on it.
  • It corrects for having looked everywhere. Search enough variables and something always looks significant. Neptune applies a family-wise correction across every test a card runs, holding the chance of showing you any false finding to 10% — and caps itself at three findings, ranked by the corrected bound rather than the flashiest number.
  • An empty card is a real answer. A finding has to clear an interval that excludes "no effect" and a minimum effect size before it's shown at all. Until then the card says so, rather than promoting the strongest noise it could find.

04 Score

A 0–100 score that shows its work.

Point those learned weights at a forecast and you get tomorrow's Conditions Score. Each factor is weighted by its own measured lift in your history, then scored on how close the forecast sits to your best band.

Crucially, the score always comes with its breakdown. You can see that today's 78 is mostly pressure and water temp — and decide for yourself whether you buy it. A black-box number you can't interrogate is just a horoscope.

  • Per-factor point contributions, sorted by weight — the score is an explanation, not a verdict.
  • Hour-by-hour curve plus a multi-day forecast strip, so you can pick the window, not just the day.
  • Alerts when a genuinely good window appears on water you fish, and a home-screen widget for the day's score.

05 · The part no other logbook does

Year Class: how old is that fish, and is it prime?

You caught a 19½-inch brown trout. Every other app records a number. Neptune works out that the fish was born in 2020, that it's six years old, that six is squarely inside the trophy window for its species — and that the same stocked cohort went into three other water bodies you can drive to.

How it gets there

  1. 1
    Length becomes age. A von Bertalanffy growth curve, parameterised per species from published age-and-growth studies, back-solves age from length.
  2. 2
    Age becomes a year class. Catch year minus age gives the fish's birth year — its cohort.
  3. 3
    The cohort gets matched. State stocking records carry a survey year and an age class; subtract them and you know when each stocked batch was born. Neptune matches your fish's year class against every record within a one-year tolerance — across water bodies, not just the one you were standing in.
  4. 4
    Prime, or not. 39 species carry a curated trophy-potential age window. If the fish's age falls inside its species' window, it's flagged PRIME.
Sourced, or absent — never invented. Most windows come from state and federal fisheries agency data (Walleye 8–15 from documented trophy ages; Muskellunge 8–15, where the average angler-caught fish is 11 years and 45 inches). Where no citable trophy age exists, the window is derived transparently from the species' own growth curve and labelled as such. A species with no curated window simply gets no badge — and true forage species like Golden Shiner are excluded on purpose, because nobody is chasing a trophy one.

Why it matters

Read a water body's future, not just its past.

If a lake got a heavy 2020 stocking and that species peaks at ages 6–12, you know something concrete about the next few seasons there. Stocking numbers alone don't tell you that; age does.

Why it matters

Find the same fish somewhere quieter.

Cross-water-body cohort matching means the well-known river isn't your only option. The same batch of fish, the same age, often went into water nobody is crowding.

Your statistics

Thirteen ways to read your own history.

The Insights tab isn't one dashboard — it's a library of 13 cards you arrange to taste, across Charts, Intelligence and Records, with your favourites pinned to the Home grid. Every one is computed from your catches alone. It used to be 22: cards that answered the same question without a denominator were retired into the ones that answer it with a rate, because two surfaces that can disagree are worse than one that can't.

Bite Window: the only card that counts the hours you caught nothing

Every other bite predictor plots when you caught fish, which mostly reveals when you go fishing. Neptune's Bite Window plots catch rate per hour fished, so the blank hours pull the line down exactly as they should. And it doesn't assume the clock is the right axis: you can read the same rate against the clock, the sun (offsets from sunrise and sunset, because crepuscular feeding tracks light, not 6 AM), the tide (offsets from the nearest high or low, offered only where the water actually resolves to a tide station), or the moon (solunar major and minor periods). Filter by species, season or water body and the denominator follows.

Conditions Window

Every condition as a response curve across its whole range, with the current reading marked on it — and the marker refuses to be drawn when today sits outside the range your log actually covers, rather than clamping to the end and implying a prediction it can't make.

Size & weight distributions

Tiny through Trophy, ranked by percentile against your own catches of that same species — so "trophy" means trophy for you, and a 20″ pickerel isn't judged against a 20″ bass.

Species breakdown

What you're catching, and how the mix shifts by season and water.

Lure performance

Which lures, sizes, and colours earn their place in the box.

Top spots

Your most productive water, ranked — with per-trip stats behind each.

Moon times

Moonrise, moonset, illumination and phase for your next trip.

Milestones & personal bests

Length and weight records per species, plus badges you actually earn — first 20″ fish, 100 catches, Night Owl, a 30-day streak.

Stocking origins

Which of your catches trace back to a stocked cohort at that water, grouped by trip or by location.

The Logbook, for people who like a spreadsheet

Trips and catches as a filterable, sortable table with 30 selectable columns — every manual field and every auto-captured condition, down to pressure trend and traffic index — plus saved views and CSV export in portable Imperial and ISO-8601.

On your lock screen, without opening the app

A home-screen widget carries the day's score and the best day ahead. Start a trip and a Live Activity puts the running timer, the water, and your catch count in the Dynamic Island.

Explore & Discover: find water like the water you already love

A similarity engine matches water bodies on their characteristics and on an observed-forage layer — what bait actually lives there, curated per state from agency reports, forum corroboration, and regional assemblage defaults. Stocking data, regulations, species and habitat profiles sit alongside it, plus a clustered map that stays smooth at tens of thousands of pins.

Where it works

Four states deep, fifteen states wide.

Logging, conditions and your own statistics work anywhere — they only need your catches and public weather data. The reference layers are being built state by state, and it's worth being precise about which is which rather than colouring in a map:

  • Full depth in NY, NJ, PA and FL — stocking history, regulations, and survey-grade forage profiles.
  • Stocking pipelines live in 10 of 15 states, drawn from each agency's own feed — ArcGIS services, JSON APIs, even per-county PDFs. Where a state publishes a schedule rather than a delivery log, it's stored as a schedule and kept out of "recently stocked" alerts.
  • Named-water forage now reaches 12 of 15 states, sourced from official survey reports rather than estimated. Where only a coarse regional estimate exists, the app badges it as exactly that instead of passing it off as a survey.
  • Regulations stay in the four deep states only, and stay blank everywhere else rather than approximate — it's the one place a wrong number has legal consequences.

Where it stops

An engine that says "I don't know".

Every inference in Neptune is gated. It is a deliberate design rule that the app degrades to silence rather than to a plausible-sounding guess.

  • A pattern whose interval still crosses "no effect" makes no claim at all, however many catches sit behind it.
  • A stocking record whose state published no fish count stays blank — never quietly counted as zero, which would rank a real stocking last.
  • A stocking record with no age class is never forced into a cohort.
  • An uncurated species gets no PRIME badge, not a guessed one.
  • A missing forecast value drops its factor from the score, silently.

Open beta

No ads. No social feed. No selling your spots.

Neptune is a private logbook that happens to be good at statistics. Your catch history exists to serve your own insights and nothing else.

And if you film yourself fishing, Incognito hides the water's name, the map and your catch pins behind a mask for the whole trip — a screen recording gives your spot away in about a second otherwise. The trip still logs exactly as it always did; the masking is display-only, and built so it structurally can't reach what gets stored.
